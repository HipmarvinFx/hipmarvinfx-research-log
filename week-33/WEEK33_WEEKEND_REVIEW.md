## LESSONS

- **Soft inflation prints don't all move the same way:** CPI in-line Wednesday was dovish (removed a tail risk rather than confirming a soft read), while PPI soft Thursday was continuation. That's two different trader behaviors — removal of bad news vs. confirmation of good news — and they both drove dollars lower, but the theta ran out by Friday. Lesson: distinguish between "relief rally" (when bad news becomes less bad, typically a one-day event) and "continuation rally" (when good news keeps coming). Relief rallies often exhaust faster.

- **A basket triggered perfectly but didn't diverge** — AUD, EUR, GBP, Gold all fired on CPI Wednesday, none of them diverged to take separate paths. Thursday's GBP-specific catalyst (UK GDP beat) didn't move GBP/USD materially away from the broader dollar move. That's the Correlation Check warning being real, not theoretical. Next time: if four ideas are genuinely correlated, size them together as one basket and manage them as such, not as six independent ideas sitting in a file.

- **Two Week 31 shorts needed to be closed three weeks ago** — both AUD and XAU have been decisively stopped since at least Wednesday, and the file has flagged both as "likely stopped" since Monday. Formal closure this week is correct, but it's late. Lesson: don't let a position sit flagged as "unconfirmed stop" for two weeks waiting for a Daily Update to source the exact breach candle. A stop that's been breached for two trading days is a stop that's breached, even if you're waiting for tick-perfect documentation.

- **No lesson on the Scenario Matrix or Price Strip accuracy** — Thursday's GBP GDP beat was a direct, sourced confirmation of Priority 5's own Scenario Matrix Branch 1 (in-line or better GDP supports the long), and that's playing out correctly. The file's thesis holds up; the execution just didn't have room to pay off.
