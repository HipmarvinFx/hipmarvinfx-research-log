# Dollar Weakness Extends to Fresh Extremes Ahead of Friday's NFP Test

## This week's view

The dollar-negative move that broke out mid-last week hasn't just held — it's extended hard, with every major pair now sitting at a fresh extreme of its own recent range. The Dollar Index closed last week at 99.802, decisively through levels it had respected for weeks, while USD/JPY and USD/CAD have both broken down sharply. On the other side, EUR/USD, GBP/USD, AUD/USD and NZD/USD have all pushed to the top of their own ranges.

This week's calendar is lighter — there's no central bank decision — but it isn't quiet. Monday's ISM print is the first real test of whether the move stabilizes, Tuesday brings the one standalone catalyst of the week in New Zealand's employment data, and Friday lands a heavy US/Canada employment cluster that's the week's genuine risk event. The open question isn't direction anymore — it's room. Several pairs are already stretched at multi-week extremes, which changes the calculus on simply riding last week's trade further.

## What's driving it

**Monday — US ISM Manufacturing PMI.** The first US data print since the dollar broke down. A beat that holds through the session would be the first real evidence the move is stabilizing rather than extending; a miss reinforces the dollar-soft backdrop the week's ideas are leaning on.

**Tuesday — New Zealand employment data.** The one genuinely standalone, pair-specific catalyst this week. Everything else below is riding the broader dollar move — this print is what would validate the NZD long on its own merits rather than on correlation alone.

**Friday — US Non-Farm Payrolls, average hourly earnings and unemployment.** The single heaviest event of the week and the real test of the dollar-weakness thesis. A hot print is the one scenario that could sharply interrupt the correlated basket below in a single session; a soft print reinforces it.

**Friday — Canada employment, same minute as NFP.** CAD's own data lands at the exact same time as the US numbers, making USD/CAD specifically a two-way, high-noise print that can't be cleanly attributed to either side without checking both.

## Correlation and zone check

Worth reading plainly rather than pair by pair: the USD/JPY short, USD/CAD short, NZD long, and the long sides of the EUR and GBP conditionals are all, at their core, the same bet — that the dollar-weakness move extending since last week keeps going. That's five pair expressions of one directional view. If Friday's payrolls print hot, all five are exposed together, not independently.

That correlation is now compounded by stretch. Four of the five continuation legs above are individually priced against their own zone logic — the shorts are selling into Discount zones, the longs are buying into Premium zones. That's not a coincidence; it's the direct consequence of the move having already run hard before this week's analysis was published. None of this makes the ideas wrong on their own, but it does mean this basket is crowded and technically stretched at the same time — which argues for disciplined sizing, not leaning in harder.

## Institutional positioning

CFTC positioning data (as of July 28, roughly five days old relative to this week's publish date) shows a consistent pattern on the two legs of this basket with the clearest breakdowns: both leveraged funds and asset managers were net short JPY and net short CAD, and adding to both, directly ahead of last week's USD/JPY and USD/CAD breakdowns. In plain terms, positioning was leaning the opposite way from the move that followed on both pairs — relevant context for how stretched those two ideas already are on a zone basis.

EUR tells a similar story in reverse: both categories moved against EUR ahead of its push to a fresh high — leveraged funds added to their short, asset managers trimmed their long. GBP and NZD positioning is more mixed, with leveraged funds and asset managers pulling in different directions on GBP specifically. Gold's COT read is unavailable this cycle — the supplied report didn't include a COMEX gold contract.
