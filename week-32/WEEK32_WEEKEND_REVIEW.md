## LESSONS

- Correlation grouping describes shared directional exposure, not synchronized outcomes — this week's "one dollar-weakness bet wearing five pair names" basket split into two solid wins, one non-event (JPY never filled), and two near-misses (EUR, GBP), despite all five nominally being the same trade.
- The week's best-performing idea was the one explicitly excluded from the correlated basket. Worth treating Proxy-standalone ideas as a genuine diversification tool going forward, not a lesser afterthought to the main thesis.
- Priority 6's single-target structure (no TP2, no trailing convention) left no predefined plan for what to do once price ran well past the stated objective (4,180 → 4,341.935 by Friday). Worth considering a second objective or trailing-stop convention for future breakout-style Proxy-standalone ideas specifically.
- **Single biggest process error this week:** multiple trade-critical decisions (NZD's fill, CAD's TP1, EUR/GBP's trigger tests) were carried across four straight days as "very likely, not confirmed" without ever being resolved to an actual tick-level source — the week closes with real money outcomes still resting on chart-shape inference rather than confirmed fills, which is a repeatable gap worth fixing before next week, not a one-off.
